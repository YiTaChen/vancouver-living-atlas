# Yaletown existing lower-concourse plan

Tafyrn & Seamora photographed the installed station evacuation plan on
18 August 2009. [Photograph IMG_1084](http://www.seataf.com/blogs/canadaline/2009-08-18/images/IMG_1084.jpg)
is part of their [opening-day walkthrough](https://canadalinephotos.blogspot.com/2009/08/2009-08-18-yaletown-roundhouse-station.html),
credited under the blog's CC BY 2.5 Canada terms. The photograph is a reference;
it is not used as a game texture.

The plan establishes a long lower concourse parallel to the island, a lateral
entrance branch, and **two separate lift cores**. The street lift lands in the
entrance branch; the platform lift is at the other end of the paid hall.
The entry switchback runs across the platform axis. The broad lower staircase
and escalator bank run along that axis in **separate, longitudinally offset
openings**. [IMG_1074](http://www.seataf.com/blogs/canadaline/2009-08-18/images/IMG_1074.jpg)
shows the standalone broad stair; IMG_1075/1077 show the separate escalator bank.
This replaces the unsupported single-shaft lift, longitudinal entry switchback
and narrow stair squeezed between two escalators.

The photographed compass, mapped indoor passage 229833347 and independently
mapped street doors constrain orientation. The approximate platform polygon
923500678 constrains horizontal registration. The 2024 walking audit supplies
57 upper and 41 lower stairs. Floor elevations, the 28/29 upper split, room
widths and shaft footprints remain proportional reconstructions, not surveyed
dimensions. Opening-era equipment is reconciled with the documented 2019
additional escalator and 2026 five-gate arrangement.

The street lift faces the public concrete approach to the southeast of its
mapped doorway. [OSM footway 988151952](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/988151952)
retains the actual door-to-outside sequence, starting at node 9133604358 and
continuing to 9133604359. Its inward bearing is approximately 315.89°;
this is the footway direction, not a surveyed rectangular shaft alignment.
[Exterior IMG_3630](http://www.seataf.com/blogs/canadaline/2009-08-07/images/IMG_3630.jpg)
and [corner detail IMG_3632](http://www.seataf.com/blogs/canadaline/2009-08-07/images/IMG_3632.jpg)
in the photographer's [7 August 2009 set](https://canadalinephotos.blogspot.com/2009/08/2009-08-07-yaletown-roundhouse-station.html)
show the separate tall glazed tower beside the stair entrance. The source keeps
the mapped street door and places the street/concourse cabin inside it, opening
towards the actual exterior footway. The tower envelope, inset pavilion portal,
shaft size and lower landing offset remain representative. The pavilion's
rectangular room enclosure is a proportional reconstruction; the retained OSM
building polygon is not claimed to be a surveyed as-built wall plan.
The returning entry escalator discharges beside the corrected lift core and
connects around its south side to the unpaid hall. Its short lower lobby and
the staggered escalator/landing opening are inferred clearances within the
photographed entrance branch. Both landing slabs are cut back to the flight;
no flat slab masks the first or last steps or creates a route through the lift.

Nominal boarding waits now occupy the clear track-edge aisles at source X±2.3m,
within their original waiting regions. This is an authored capsule-clear
position, not a surveyed pavement marking. Raw origins must pass support and
capsule checks without `safePoint` relocation, and the walker must move towards
each unchanged sill and return. Train stops, carriage-local door coordinates,
sills, platform edges, rails and threshold bridges are unchanged. The dedicated
regression also walks the actual lift portal in both directions, keeps solid
jambs, and distinguishes the standalone 41-step stair from the two lower
escalators.

The standalone platform stair also retains the stainless central handrail and
posts visible in IMG_1074. The physical stair remains 2.9 m wide with 41 risers;
its navigation endpoints use a side lane 0.7 m from the physical centre so the
walker never passes through the divider. Rail height, post spacing and lane
offset are proportional reconstructions. The rail and every post have physical
colliders. Capture viewpoints project onto that actual navigation line while
keeping the real tread height.

The south platform name/arrival-board pair occupies a flat end bay at source
Z 20.4 m (arrival board at 21.7 m), beyond the stair's Z 14 m lower endpoint and
the separate lift core at Z 18 m. The northern pair remains at Z −19 m. The old
south positions, Z 9/10.22 m, placed signs through the sloping staircase and are
rejected by the regression. Both retained pairs are checked against every
actual flight and have standing clearance over the platform. These authored
sign offsets are not claimed to be surveyed locations.
