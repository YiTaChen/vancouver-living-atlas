# Burrard and Granville: circulation and interior evidence

Reviewed 2026-10-23. Both stations' train-facing assemblies are rigidly aligned
to approximate OSM platform centres relative to independently mapped street doors. Platform lengths and car-local door transforms are preserved. Burrard’s train-facing
assembly receives one shared 5 m downward section correction; all tracks, floors,
station door points, exclusions and bridges move together. Street-to-hall placement, corridor lengths, bay widths,
stair dimensions and station depth are **representative game geometry**, not an
as-built survey. This evidence supports circulation relationships and visible
architectural character. It does not certify exact dimensions.

## Required reconstruction relationships

### Burrard

The unpaid glass ticket atrium is shared by the park/Burrard entrances and the mall
connections. Ticket machines remain in this atrium; the primary faregate line is
in the lower access hall beside the inbound platform. The separate street-level
elevator house has its own gate line. The street approach has one up escalator next to a substantial
staircase. From the atrium, a bank of three escalators descends into the
enclosed underground access hall and its faregate line. The two single-track platforms occupy
different heights. The lower outbound platform requires the additional short
vertical connector; the five-escalator count is one street escalator, three
main-bank escalators and one inter-platform escalator.

Stairs and all escalator banks must have continuous sidewalls, landing walls,
rails and physical soffits. The bright glass atrium should remain distinct from
the low, enclosed tunnel areas. Use the curved tunnel-wall and ceiling treatment
on both platform levels. Architectural roofs may fade for the game camera, while
collisions and enclosure remain present.

### Granville

Granville/Seymour entries reach the original ticket hall. The Bay corridor has a
short paired escalator connection. The ticket hall then uses the characteristic
three long parallel escalators in a single enclosed vaulted shaft. At its foot,
the circulation hub reaches the upper Waterfront-bound platform and the short
stair/escalator connection to the lower outbound platform.

The Dunsmuir/Hudson entrance is a separate circulation branch: its unpaid street
hall leads down three escalators to the tiled faregate hall beside the elevator.
The gate line belongs below the escalators, as visible in the actual photographs. It must not simply end
in a decorative lobby or be folded into the original long bank. A playable
accessible route must reach both stacked platforms.

The implemented Dunsmuir branch uses three parallel main escalators. This
grouping was verified by visually inspecting TransLink's December 2023 shaft
photograph, which is explicitly captioned as Granville's Dunsmuir exit. Its tall
rectangular tiled enclosure is distinct from the original Big 3's round acoustic
vault. A lower-platform stair and elevator connect this branch's platform halls;
the precise stair routing and internal turns remain representative. The original
bank's three parallel 35 m escalators, Bay pair and short inter-platform escalator
are separately evidenced.

## Acceptance observations and uncertainty

- Retain both 74 m platform lengths and the exact rigid relationship between
  tracks, stop anchors, station-side sill/edge/waiting points and exclusions.
  Both stations are rigidly translated to their mapped relative locations; car-local
  door transforms must never receive that translation.
- The upper and lower platforms must stay separate; there is no same-level
  cross-track island platform.
- Count visible escalators by their actual branch. Granville's six replaced
  original escalators and three Dunsmuir escalators must not be conflated.
- Verify the original entrance → ticketing/faregates → enclosed long descent →
  platform route, plus the short connector to the other platform. Dunsmuir uses
  the opposite faregate sequence: unpaid street hall → triple descent → gate line
  beside the elevator → platform access.
- Wall/soffit continuity and collision enclosure are acceptance requirements;
  roof transparency only changes rendering.
- Exact compass orientation of the subterranean branch junctions, every internal
  bend, all measured widths and actual floor elevations remain unverified here.
  They must not be advertised as survey-accurate.
- Contemporary elevator works at Burrard are temporary construction conditions;
  the reconstructed normal route models the permanent elevator connection.

## Physical verification

`node --test tests/dunsmuir-circulation.test.mjs` passes eight tests against the
actual `SkyTrainStationOwner` pedestrian runtime with `openWorld=false`:

- Every authored stair and all five Burrard / nine Granville escalators are
  traversed in both directions in 75 mm pedestrian steps.
- Each original entrance route passes the ticket hall and long bank, reaches the
  upper platform, then uses the short connector to the lower platform.
- Seymour's stair/passage, the unpaid Hudson Mall connection, the independent
  Dunsmuir descent and both Dunsmuir platform paths are continuously walkable.
- Both 74 m platform polygons and every train/door anchor retain their rigid
  relative coordinates. Each station uses one shared horizontal calibration shift,
  with every car-local offset and quaternion unchanged.

The verification found and repaired underlying landing polygons that otherwise
trapped a reverse walker below visible stairs. Stair footprints are now cut from
both the support triangles and visible slabs. Bank walls follow the shaft slope
in short sections, preventing a deep shaft's bounding wall from blocking an
unrelated corridor above it. Corner passage walls have real door apertures.

Granville's three street portals align to their declared reference positions in
the runtime frame. Granville Street and Dunsmuir use mapped OSM doors; Seymour
uses the independently qualified, photo-supported inferred facade described
below. This verifies the stated alignment, without certifying those evidence
types as equally precise or the underground geometry as a measured survey.

## Burrard sunken-park section correction

[The Vancouver Public Space Network’s first-hand site article and photos](https://vancouverpublicspace.ca/2018/01/29/art-phillips-park-a-small-space-in-a-pivotal-location/)
describe and visibly show the concourse below the street, accessed by landscaped
public stairs. [OSM stair way139571580](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/139571580)
records 27 steps. Its northwestern head is the street handoff; node1529901638
is the lower sunken-plaza endpoint and rear door, not the top of a second indoor
stair. The source uses three nine-step flights and their intermediate landings,
then a same-level plaza-to-atrium door.

The coarse city DEM resolves only about 1 m between those stair endpoints, which
would imply implausible 3.8 cm risers. The reconstructed section instead keeps the
actual 27-step count, street-head DEM, and normal approximately 16.7 cm risers,
placing the atrium at representative source Y−6.5 m. The main street stairs
extend into that atrium. Both boarding assemblies shift downward by 5 m together
(upper−14 / lower−20 m), leaving a plausible main triple-bank slope of roughly
30 degrees. These underground elevations are explicitly inferred, not survey
measurements.

[Melville-labelled node5464027763](https://www.openstreetmap.org/node/5464027763)
is an intermediate point in indoor underground Royal Centre corridor
[way567745480](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/567745480). It must not be
turned into a fictitious public street stair or georeferenced as a surface door.
The model keeps the enclosed mall connection and omits that unsupported external
entrance.

Granville's source Dunsmuir landing uses the relative DEM datum −2.07414 m.
Seymour's retained stair-head landing is +1.21093 m; its inferred facade's DEM
sample gives +1.23363015 m. The unpaid Hudson mall connection accommodates the
internal landing difference over a gentle, enclosed 50 m grade
(about 4.15%), with continuous walking triangles, visible slab, sidewalls and
soffit. Exact hidden mall dimensions remain representative.

The Dunsmuir street doorway points southwest (source −X), consistent with
OSM footway138641319. Its independently mapped sequence is entry stair
way138641324 → level −1 passage138641331 → north/east turn138641330 →
shaft direction138641320 (approximately source X61.46, Z−84.43 to Z−58.74).
The reconstruction now follows that sequence, rather than placing a parallel
triple shaft east of the real door and sending the passenger in the wrong
direction. The first descent is inferred as 3 m; OSM level tags do not survey
that elevation or prove how much of a tagged stair way is landing.

The original Big 3 retains its photographed acoustic vault and published 35 m
sloping length in a separate adjacent shaft. Its hidden mall bend, northern
head hall and exact parallel shaft offset remain representative. The model does
not describe those unmarked dimensions as measured OSM architecture. The upper
Seymour/mall passage has its own enclosed ramp and corner landing, clear of the
Big 3 lanes. Dunsmuir's street landing also has a step-free branch to that upper
mall passage and the same three-stop lift, so its access route never requires
the new entry stairs.

The Granville original ticket hall retains the photographed round stainless
column character; the Dunsmuir halls retain square tiled columns. These are
concourse supports, with no fabricated pillars inserted in the narrow platform
vaults. Their reconstructed diameters, selected positions and heights are
representative photo-based geometry. The original round support uses sixteen
closed panel faces within the maintained box renderer; each panel has the same
physical collider as its rendered surface. Positions keep the faregate gaps,
stair heads, lift portals and circulation corners clear and are physically
replayed after the change.

## Seymour street recess and right-turn stair

[Michelle Marie Whitfield's 2013 University of Washington thesis, _Turn Left at the Station_](https://digital.lib.washington.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/e7c27087-87fd-476e-ab80-87d30d24c2ac/content),
Figure 59 (printed page 59, PDF page 73), is a first-hand photograph of the
Seymour entrance. It shows a broad street-facing opening, shallow tiled recess,
ceiling and red guarding; the stairs descend to the right after entering from
the sidewalk. The public doorway faces Seymour Street toward the southeast.
Passengers enter northwest, then turn northeast down the stair. The street
approach must not run southwest through the solid Bay building as if the
underground footway were a surveyed street door.

OSM way138641322 constrains the stair-head location and its northeast axis.
The reconstructed street-facing door is placed 7 m southeast of that stair
head to meet the current Bay-building footprint edge and sidewalk; that facade
coordinate, recess depth and 6 m width are **inferred** from the photo and
building footprint. They are not an OSM doorway measurement. Source metadata
retains both the mapped stair head and separately qualified inferred street
door. The stair itself keeps its independently mapped position and direction.

The inferred Seymour facade's current DEM sample is 22.7 mm above the retained
stair-head landing. A separate 1 m deep doorway slab expresses that small
threshold explicitly. The real staircase and its vertical section remain fixed.
The round column is a compound support: its inner core and sixteen stainless
panels all have matching real colliders. A dedicated eight-direction capsule
sweep verifies the complete column and reverse movement, while the independent
rectangular-column checks cover both Dunsmuir square supports.
