The new roof sequence deforms the building over approximately 2.6 simulated seconds. The failing support carries the rocket through approximately 1.5 seconds of settling before release, with thrust shut down; these intervals can overlap. The vehicle then enters `falling`, rotates under the production simulation and reaches an actual city/ground impact. A complete, unstaged rocket breaks into its six existing model sections, which tumble and settle; after staging, only the remaining upper-stage sections belong to that vehicle. Fuel and impact conditions can produce a later explosion, which applies another impulse to the existing wreckage. An impact need not explode.

Nearby forest flames feed local thermal exposure into the vehicle. Heat accumulation and seeded vulnerability can result in no onboard fire, a fire that cools and extinguishes, or a continuing internal fire that ruptures the vehicle after liftoff. Visible body fire remains attached to the moving rocket, then to its wreckage. An airborne fire warning reports vehicle heat while guidance, throttle and abort remain available until vehicle loss.

An accident automatically switches a first-person camera to chase view. The camera follows the rocket/wreckage and checks nearby city geometry for an unobstructed observation ray, damping the chosen angle rather than snapping past a neighbouring building.
