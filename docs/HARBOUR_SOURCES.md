# Vancouver harbour simulation: sources and asset notes

Verified **4 September 2026**. This package combines original OpenStreetMap facility geometry with original, simplified animation paths. It contains **no recorded AIS/ADS-B track, approved flight procedure, nautical route, existing 3D model, or actual boat-occupancy record**. The destination names are factual; the repeated near-harbour movements are a scene demonstration.

**Helicopter destinations:** Helijet's [Summer 2026 timetable](https://helijet.com/SummerSked2026), linked from its [scheduled flights page](https://helijet.com/scheduled-airline/), expressly lists Vancouver Harbour ↔ Victoria Harbour and Vancouver Harbour ↔ Nanaimo Harbour from 1 June 2026. It identifies Sikorsky S76 service; published sector times are 35 minutes to Victoria and 20 minutes to Nanaimo. The local scene route can represent either destination, but its duration and path are illustrative.

**Seaplane destinations:** the operator's [2026 route guide](https://harbourairteam.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/158000407774-harbour-air-routes), linked from [Harbour Air contact/support](https://harbourair.com/contact-us/), confirms Vancouver Harbour ↔ Victoria Harbour and Vancouver Harbour ↔ Nanaimo Harbour as year-round routes. These do not use Helijet's destination facilities: Harbour Air's Victoria terminal is at 950 Wharf Street, while Helijet uses 79 Dallas Road; Harbour Air's Nanaimo terminal is at 90 Front Street, while Helijet uses 100 Port Drive. The simulation does not place the remote terminals outside its scene bounds.

**Cruise destinations and seasonality:** use “Alaska / Inside Passage” as the main destination label. [Royal Caribbean's Alaska page](https://www.royalcaribbean.com/alaska-cruises) identifies Vancouver round trips through the Inside Passage from May through September. [Princess's 2026–27 announcement](https://gb-www.princess.com/en-int/news/news-releases/2024/12/experience-the-wonders-of-mexico-hawaii-and-california-with-princess-cruises-sailings-from-the-west-coast) also identifies September 2026 Vancouver → Los Angeles departures. [Destination Vancouver](https://www.destinationvancouver.com/plan-your-trip/cruise-vancouver) describes additional Hawaii and California options and year-round cruise departures overall; do not describe the whole port as exclusively a summer operation.

**Geographic exit:** [BC Geographical Names](https://apps.gov.bc.ca/pub/bcgnws/names/12769.html) identifies First Narrows as the entrance to Burrard Inlet, crossed by Lions Gate Bridge. [Parks Canada's bridge record](https://www.pc.gc.ca/apps/dfhd/page_nhs_eng.aspx?id=11273) places the bridge at the port entrance between Stanley Park and the North Shore. The [Port of Vancouver information guide](https://www.portvancouver.com/media/documents/port-information-guide-0) provides the First Narrows context; its direct fetch was blocked by robots during this research, so no new operational rules were extracted from it. The scene's geographic path goes through this opening; its exact centreline, turns, speed, bridge timing, and departure sequence are authored, not port navigation instructions.

## Final assets and schema

- **`harbour-routes.json`**: `{schemaVersion, coordinateSystem:'EPSG:4326', routes:[...]}`. Each route has `id`, `kind` (`cruise`, `seaplane`, `helicopter`), `coordinates:[[lon,lat],...]`, matching `altitudesM`, `sourceIds`, destination ID(s), `displaySimplified:true`, and `loop:false`. Original control points are retained in `controlCoordinates` / `controlAltitudesM`. Final paths are original centripetal curves sampled at approximately 20 m. Prefer interpolation through these dense points rather than another large smoothing pass.
- **`harbour-sites.json`**: seven facility/staging anchors, three groups of verified destination metadata, and **48 illustrative moorings** (36 Coal Harbour, 12 Bayshore West). Use `illustrativeMoorings` for boat placement; marina centroids are label anchors. Every berth records its actual source pier ID, an authored offset coordinate, heading clockwise from true north, and conservative boat length/width. Occupancy is decorative, not observed.
- **`harbour-piers.json`** (published name; prepared source `piers.geojson`): 215 OSM piers, preserving line versus area geometry. For collision tests, line piers were buffered by 0.7 m each side as a stated display-width estimate. Polygon piers retain their footprint and holes.
- **`marinas.geojson`**: six source marina polygons; only Coal Harbour Marina and Bayshore West are used for generated private-boat placements.
- **`aviation-facilities.geojson`**: source heliport, helipad, and floating-terminal geometry. Some other helipads belong to unrelated/private facilities; use the selected Helijet pad ID for scheduled-service illustration.
- **`calm-water-zones.geojson`**: existing project OSM polygons for Lost Lagoon and Beaver Lake. Both receive an art-directed calm-water preset; the geometry is geographic, the suggested wave amplitude is not a weather measurement.
- `harbour-features.geojson`, `osm-harbour-raw.json`, and `harbour-query.overpass` retain combined prepared data, raw Overpass response, and exact query. Raw snapshot: **2026-09-04 23:37:51 UTC**. The prepared set contains 228 features.

All route points remain inside the intended model domain: longitude **−123.23…−123.087**, latitude **49.258…49.337**. The near-port cruise and flight routes end offshore within this domain; they do not draw an invented full journey to Victoria, Nanaimo, or Alaska. Fade/reset or create a separate return animation at those limits. `altitudesM` are art-directed **absolute metres above scene sea level**, not heights to add to sampled terrain. Adjust the initial helicopter anchor to the modeled helipad surface if necessary, while preserving the explicitly estimated airborne profile. Seaplane staging is on the water outside the floating terminal.

## Geometry verification and wave interpretation

`harbour-preparation-qa.json` verifies staging points and source geometry. All 48 mooring rectangles fit inside their marina polygons without intersecting scene land, source piers, or each other. The open-water player start `[-123.1226,49.2930]` is clear of land and piers; it is a scene start, not a published launch site.

`harbour-route-qa.json` verifies final resampled curves. The cruise uses a conservative **260 m × 42 m** full rectangular vessel envelope sampled every approximately 5 m, including the docked first pose. Checks include detailed/core plus context coastlines, all source pier geometry, current building and landmark footprints, and the exact plan rectangle of the original Canada Place scene deck. It deliberately continues along the berth before turning so the stern clears the pier. The seaplane's initial water phase is checked with a **12 m half-width** corridor, wider than its fuselage to account for wings. Runtime should still validate the actual chosen meshes and interpolation; these tests are scene geometry checks, not hydrographic clearance certification.

For appearance, use longer, stronger waves on open bay water, gentler motion in sheltered harbour/marina water, and almost-flat ripples inside the two lake polygons. These are controlled visual presets, not live wind, tide, depth, or wave measurements. Render boats and sample their heave/pitch from the same water-height function so they remain attached to the visible surface.

## Reproduction and licensing

The local research used Python/Shapely for source preparation and full-rectangle shoreline sweeps, and Three.js for centripetal route resampling. The repository commits the prepared geographic assets, retained control coordinates and QA report. `npm test` verifies the published routes against the application's current collision geometry, including original Canada Place decks and bridge columns. Asset metadata preserves source IDs, timestamps and the distinction between measured facilities and authored demonstration movement.
