# Tesla Roadster Rocket — October 2026 integration

A separate player vehicle and travel button, alongside Orbit / Walk / Drive / Boat / Fly. It does not replace the existing roadster, aircraft, traffic, buses or trains.

The independent Tesla is exempt from police speed enforcement. Entering Tesla clears any pending or active roadster stop, including its patrol/officer and speech. Ordinary Drive retains its existing enforcement thresholds; this exemption does not change either vehicle's physical maximum speed.

## Reference and scope

The reference is the redesigned white, sharply faceted Roadster revealed in October 2026, including copper/gold glazing, raised fenders, concealed lamps, full-width rear light, yoke cabin, active aero and perimeter thrusters.

Primary references:

- [Tesla Roadster](https://www.tesla.com/roadster)
- [Tesla event replay](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qt5p-_tE8d0)
- [Tesla — The Ramp](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7p5LxFdc-Qg)
- [Tesla — The Kickflip](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r5vsHHPVhJg)

The independent [Tesla Roadster simulator](https://github.com/eduardmur/tesla-roadster-simulator) was also inspected for its compact body rendering and bounded thrust effects. No code, mesh, image or audio from that project was copied into this integration.

The mesh is original editable TypeScript geometry, authored against public visual references. There is no imported proprietary CAD, downloaded fan model, official texture or claimed Tesla endorsement. Dimensions, collision envelope and cockpit eye position are visual modelling estimates, not surveyed measurements. The source records uncertain hidden mechanical details. This is a reference-based game representation, not an exact CAD reproduction.

Unlimited sustained flight, wall/roof parking and the Vortex Jump throughout Vancouver are requested game mechanics; they are not claims about the production car's flight endurance, range or wall-driving certification.

## Controls

| Action | Desktop | Touch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Drive / steer | W/S or arrows; A/D | Left joystick |
| Jump, then climb | Space; hold to climb | Hold upward thrust |
| Vortex Jump / 旋風飛躍 | V | Tap Vortex Jump |
| Descend | Q (Ctrl also accepted) | Hold downward thrust |
| Boost | Shift | Hold boost |
| Brake | B | Hold brake |
| Wall Grip (default) / Free Drive | E | Wall Grip mode toggle |
| Optional manual park / release | P | Manual park toggle |
| Flight assist | F | Flight assist toggle |
| Chase / cockpit | C | Camera button |
| Look | Drag scene | Drag scene with another finger |
| Chase distance / enter cockpit | Wheel / zoom | Pinch |
| Recover to a verified road | R | Recovery in options |

**Wall Grip** (壁面吸附) is the default mode. Drive into a supported facade to climb automatically; the vehicle checks a swept full-body transition rather than snapping its rear through the pavement. Release the throttle to decelerate, or hold B / the brake button to stop sooner. Once stopped on the wall, automatic hold keeps the car in place without pressing P. Fresh throttle or boost resumes wall driving. The hold revalidates support and body clearance; it does not preserve an attachment after its supporting geometry disappears.

Brake takes priority over throttle and steering while held at zero wall speed. Release the brake and drive to leave automatic hold.

**Free Drive** (自由行駛) disables automatic wall attachment. Press E or tap the mode toggle to switch. Switching it off while attached uses the collision-checked outward departure before levelling into flight. Space also detaches. P remains available for optional manual parking, including queueing it during an attachment, but is not required for wall stopping. Third-person camera retains world up; the cockpit uses the full body orientation. Collision checks keep the chase camera outside buildings.

The selected wall mode survives safe-road recovery and leaving/re-entering Tesla during the same session. A newly created controller starts in Wall Grip. The HUD distinguishes **Auto hold** from optional **Manual park**; pressing the accelerator releases either without a separate unpark action.

**Automatic wall → roof:** Keep driving upward in Wall Grip. Before the front of the car loses its full facade support, the vehicle looks for the adjoining actual roof. A proved route retreats outside the facade, follows a rounded rotating arc around the real roof edge, then moves inward and settles onto the roof. Holding throttle continues roof driving; releasing it during the transfer reduces arrival speed, and stopping on the roof automatically holds the exact pose. The HUD distinguishes roof driving and roof holding. Free Drive still permits ordinary roof driving/parking after arrival. Roof holes, registered building geometry and visible roof-detail envelopes remain physical constraints. A blocked route retains collision-safe wall behavior and the existing safe outward departure at an unsupported edge.

**Vortex Jump:** V or the ninth touch button fires an original jet-assisted vault with one 360° roll about the car's forward axis. It first clears its actual supporting surface and safely aligns upright, then runs a nominal 1.4-second lift/roll/settling phase. A ground launch finishes in natural descent for an upright landing; an airborne launch restores the previous flight-assist choice. The move uses the full rotating collision body at each small step, so an obstacle interrupts it rather than granting passage. B cancels and brakes; Space, P, F and recovery retain their normal interrupt roles. The button shows active progress and prevents retriggering during the maneuver or a pending surface transition. There is no fuel, stamina or tank counter.

Desktop controls include a compact HUD and optional help. Touch controls support independent pointer capture so steering and thrust can be held together. Mobile options contain camera, recovery and car quality. Opening an obstructing panel suspends this player's physics and clears held inputs. Closing it requires a fresh key press; old OS repeat events cannot resume acceleration.

## Quality and resource bounds

Car geometry has its own Low / Medium / High / Auto selection. A manual selection remains forced independently of the city's rendering quality.

| Tier | Visible model triangles | Beauty meshes |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Low | 10,590 | 35 |
| Medium | 27,140 | 35 |
| High | 58,922 | 35 |

The ten-port gas effect adds one draw and 160 triangles when visible. All three cached tiers plus the effect retain 6,959,616 bytes of typed geometry buffers, 13 shared material objects and zero textures in the CPU inventory. This excludes Three.js object overhead and driver allocations; it is not a whole-city GPU-memory claim.

Vortex Jump pulses the same ten gas instances with bounded intensity and scale. It adds no particle pool, texture, geometry tier, material or effect draw. Low / Medium / High / Auto remain independent of the maneuver's physical state.

Auto chooses Low on the compatible renderer or during sustained fast travel, Medium for ordinary street use, and High for cockpit detail where the city budget permits. A candidate must remain stable for 2.2 seconds before an automatic change. At most three geometry tiers are retained; only one is visible. Shared materials and a fixed-size gas effect avoid per-frame mesh, material or particle allocation. Geometry, animation state and simulation are separate, so a tier change does not reset vehicle motion.

The existing city Auto controller receives actual Tesla speed and a ground/flight context. It continues to own city pixel resolution and scenery admission. Car quality does not disable the existing city Auto/manual setting.

## Physical world and lifecycle

- Continuous oriented-body sweeps retain the complete physical vehicle and bounded angular substeps. Model-backed ordinary ground proves all four actual tyre contacts and continuous accepted floor beneath their authored support rectangle; body overhang can extend beyond that support area. Roofs, walls, protected floors and legacy shapes retain their complete body-footprint support rules.
- Actual rendered asphalt, ordinary sidewalks and terrain triangles. A 13 cm paving step is represented as physical geometry; tyre fitting does not grant permission to pass a curb that overlaps the conservative body envelope.
- Ordinary-road driving fits the car to four authored tyre contacts across the actual triangle mesh. The fitted chassis pose is separate from the physical contact face, so a triangle's real normal and identity are never overwritten. Pose corrections remain bounded and collision swept. Three conservative underside sections retain 8 / 12 / 15 cm rear / centre / front clearance; clipping every non-wheel triangle against the section boundaries verifies these bounds across all three LODs. The separate 5 cm wheel-fit allowance cannot enlarge body clearance. These are authored model/game data, not a claim about the real vehicle's suspension. Walls, roofs, protected floors and other obstacles retain the full body envelope.
- On ordinary ground, footprint probes and analytic edge coverage follow the actual tyre plane and the min/max authored tyre positions, including their local offset. This keeps a single source triangle's physical normal separate from the fitted driving attitude at a crest or sag. Every tyre must still meet its existing 5 cm residual bound; holes inside the tyre support rectangle, missing wheel support, excluded layers and actual body obstacles remain rejected.
- At a finite crest, ordinary model support uses the real floor below the chassis rather than rejecting the infinite extension of a triangle that has already ended. Complete finite-body clearance remains a separate mandatory proof. If the preferred least-squares road attitude cannot fit, a fallback preserves the requested orientation and intersects all four existing wheel-travel height intervals with the bounded adjustment budget. Exact physical collision intervals choose the nearest legal height; final support, body clearance and complete movement sweeps must still pass.
- Shifted underside sections can dismiss an angular-padding false hit only after analytic projections prove separation for every interval of the full continuous arc. Refinement is capped at depth 6 / 127 nodes; an unresolved interval keeps the original collision. Interior triangle crossings and initial overlap remain blocking. A rejected refit may retain the requested frame only after fresh floor, complete route and resting-body proofs.
- Manual parking revalidates fresh support and complete body clearance. A newly overlapping physical source revokes the parked state and reports the collision without moving the vehicle.
- At a road crease, a bounded vertical chassis adjustment may use the remaining wheel-fit allowance to clear the conservative underside collision envelope; the source triangles and body clearances remain unchanged. Launch first clears the proven road vertically with fixed orientation and horizontal position, then resumes flight motion once the full envelope is clear. This short departure revalidates its original road support and preserves overhead and other obstacles.
- Vortex launch captures the freshly proved gentle floor tangent as momentum, preserving its relative-road arc on a grade. After the original bounded ground departure clears the full body, a blocked alignment may add at most 35 cm of fully swept vertical preparation with unchanged orientation and no ignored surfaces. An overhead obstacle or exhausted clearance budget cancels the move. Graded model-backed ground landings propose a 1 mm outward separation to avoid micron-scale Float32 triangle seams; fresh support and every whole/partial route sweep still apply. Flat ground, legacy shapes, roofs and walls retain their exact landing targets.
- Building footprints, holes, heights and pitched roof triangles come from the city data. Landmarks are obstacles rather than invented flat roofs.
- Ready, visible architectural roof-detail instances also contribute conservative transformed bounding-box obstacles. They reuse the rendered instance transforms; hidden or unadmitted appearance plans do not create invisible equipment. The local collision overlay caches at most four roof records and generates triangle faces lazily. These envelopes can be wider than an equipment model's exact triangles. A detail that streams in beneath an already occupied roof can block movement; safe-road recovery remains available rather than silently moving the car.
- Protected deck identities/layers and drive permissions remain explicit. Recovery/spawn uses verified asphalt or a drive-approved protected floor, never an arbitrary terrain height. Entry and recovery prefer a freshly checked 20 m vertical vehicle corridor; this skips an actual tree-covered road found by the default-overview smoke test. Ordinary ground uses its strict first 35 cm departure proof, followed by a full-body no-ignore sweep. Protected floors use no-ignore proof throughout. Street entry and recovery retain the driveable floor identity/layer. A verified covered road remains a fallback when no clear candidate exists; this is a placement preference, not permission to pass through overhead obstacles.
- Water barriers follow actual sea/pond footprints and levels; narrow trunks and elevated crowns preserve real clearance under broadleaf street trees.
- Local 32 m collision tiles use bounded caches. Broad terrain/water sources are indexed lazily; the full city's triangles are not generated at mode entry.
- Collision tiles also use height bands, so a high-altitude query does not prepare road/tree triangles far below the car. Ordinary connected road grades have a bounded suspension envelope; protected decks and wall/roof support retain strict geometry checks.
- No fuel, pressure-tank or stamina counter. A 1,800 m altitude ceiling retains camera/query margin within a 2,000 m world envelope. Recovery returns to a verified road.
- Vehicle input, camera and mode ownership are released before placement, aircraft, bus visits, landmark focus or Orbit. Disposal releases all cached tiers, materials and listeners.
